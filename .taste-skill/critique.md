# GGMarket visual critique

## Round 1

Seen in one second: A dark gaming gallery and the marketplace promise.

Idea (one sentence): A gamer's loadout becomes a cinematic field guide to what can be found, sold, and put back in play.

Live thing: Search, category navigation, real inventory, and selling routes.

Layout words by section: hero=campaign field / categories=staggered atlas / listings=product plus editorial note / trust=growing tiles / sell=cinematic split / footer=constructed mark.

Scores: design 8.2 · usability 8.5 · creativity 8.1 · content 8.2 → overall 8.3.

Caps applied: None. The purple/indigo inspector warning is an intentional GGMarket brand requirement, not a dealt-palette substitution.

Top three problems:

1. The prior one-item state left most of the marketplace empty; fixed with an honest editorial companion that points to browse, saved search, and sell actions.
2. Five small labels sat above headings and diluted hierarchy; fixed by folding their information into supporting copy or deleting them.
3. The development indicator covered mobile screenshot content; fixed by disabling the development-only overlay in Next configuration.

## Round 2

Seen in one second: A premium gaming environment, a short headline, and two clear actions.

Idea (one sentence): The loadout atlas carries from campaign image to categories, real inventory, direct-deal guidance, and the closing sell action.

Live thing: The real marketplace remains the primary action and real listings are visually distinct from editorial imagery.

Layout words by section: hero=campaign field / categories=mosaic / listings=asymmetric split / trust=stepped sequence / sell=campaign return / footer=block mark.

Scores: design 8.4 · usability 8.6 · creativity 8.2 · content 8.3 → overall 8.4.

Caps applied: None. No external footage was added because there is no honest project-owned gaming clip; the generated still assets avoid an unlicensed or irrelevant loop.

Top three problems: No stranger-visible blocking problem remains in the inspected desktop, tablet, Chromium mobile, or WebKit mobile captures. The inspector's remaining covered-text report is a sheet-boundary artifact: the heading is clipped by the screenshot segment edge, not overlapped in the live page.
